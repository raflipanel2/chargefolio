How to recycle lithium-ion and household batteries safely

The correct destination depends on the chemistry and condition of the battery.

ChargeFolio Editorial DeskReviewed 11 Nov 2026294 core words

Why ordinary bins are wrong for lithium-ion

Lithium-ion batteries can retain energy after they seem used up. Damaged terminals or crushing during waste collection can create fire risks. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency advises against placing lithium-ion batteries in household trash or ordinary curbside recycling.

Instead, use a battery collection center, participating retailer or household hazardous waste program that accepts the battery type. Collection options vary greatly by jurisdiction.

Prepare an intact used battery

Follow the destination facility’s requirements. The EPA recommends isolating terminals with non-conductive tape or placing batteries separately in plastic bags to avoid short circuits. Do not cover essential safety information if the receiving program requires it.

Keep batteries protected from impacts or metal objects while awaiting a suitable drop-off. If the product has a non-removable cell, follow the manufacturer’s recycling guidance for the entire device.

Handle damaged items separately

A swollen, leaking, smoking or heat-damaged battery may not be accepted by a standard collection point. Call the manufacturer, the local hazardous waste authority or a specialist before transport; do not treat a damaged pack as routine recycling.

If there is active smoke, fire or imminent danger, follow local emergency guidance rather than moving the item yourself.

Separate chemistries

AA alkaline, rechargeable NiMH, button cells, lithium metal and lithium-ion products have different disposal and handling considerations. Read the battery markings and local program rules rather than sorting by shape alone.

For large packs in e-bikes or equipment, the manufacturer or a specialist may be the appropriate first contact. Never disassemble a large pack to fit a household collection container.

Questions readers ask

Can lithium-ion batteries go with ordinary curbside recyclables?

The U.S. EPA advises against it. These batteries can start fires if damaged in recycling trucks or sorting machinery. Use a battery-specific collection point or household hazardous waste program that accepts the battery type and condition, and follow its preparation requirements.

What should I do with a phone that has a sealed battery?

Do not open it solely to remove the battery for recycling. Contact an electronics recycler or the device manufacturer about accepting the entire device. Erase personal data using the vendor’s supported process if the device can be safely powered on, and prioritize safety if damaged.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A retired undamaged phone power bank should go to an appropriate battery collection program with terminals protected, not into a mixed paper-and-plastic recycling bin.

Practical checklist

  • Identify chemistry and battery condition.
  • Use a compatible collection or hazardous waste program.
  • Protect intact used terminals from shorts.
  • Seek special handling guidance for damaged packs.
