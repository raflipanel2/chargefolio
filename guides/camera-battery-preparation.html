Camera batteries: packing, backups, and storage

Photo days fail when spare batteries are flat, unprotected or packed incorrectly.

ChargeFolio Editorial DeskReviewed 11 Nov 2026309 core words

Start with actual shooting conditions

Cold conditions, continuous video, image stabilization and high screen brightness can change camera runtime. Plan spares based on the actual shooting day rather than a laboratory shot count alone. For a professional event, test the equipment in a representative session beforehand.

Use the camera maker’s approved batteries and chargers. Third-party products may have different safety features, reporting behavior and capacity.

Protect loose packs

Keep spare battery terminals insulated by their cases or the manufacturer’s approved protection. A spare pack loose with coins or keys is vulnerable to a short circuit. Organize camera accessories so heavy lenses cannot crush batteries.

Never travel with swollen, damaged or recalled lithium batteries. Identify the Wh rating on larger packs before arranging air travel.

Airline preparation

The FAA states that spare lithium-ion camera batteries go in carry-on baggage and must be protected from short circuits. Packs up to 100 Wh fall within the ordinary listed category; packs above 100 Wh and up to 160 Wh generally need carrier approval and have quantity limits. Airline and international rules can be stricter.

When a carry-on is gate checked, move the spare batteries or power banks into the cabin as required. Verify the policy for your specific route and carrier before leaving home.

Between assignments

Store spare batteries according to the maker’s temperature and charge guidance. Keep a simple date label and inspect for physical damage before reuse. A pack that behaves strangely should be serviced or replaced, not forced to keep charging.

For disposal, the EPA recommends separate collection for used lithium-ion batteries, not household trash or curbside recycling.

Questions readers ask

Can camera spares go in checked luggage?

For ordinary spare lithium-ion camera batteries, FAA guidance says no: spares should travel in carry-on baggage with terminals protected from short circuits. Capacity and quantity limits depend on Wh rating and carrier policy. When a cabin bag is gate checked, remove spares first.

How should I prepare for a winter outdoor shoot?

Read the maker’s operating-temperature range, keep spare batteries protected from physical damage and plan additional charged spares based on real testing. Never apply direct uncontrolled heat to a pack. A lower cold-weather runtime does not necessarily prove permanent battery failure.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A videographer carrying two spare 80 Wh camera packs in protective cases should still verify carrier rules and keep them in cabin baggage, not place them with checked lenses.

Practical checklist

  • Review camera-maker compatibility guidance.
  • Pack each spare against short circuits and impacts.
  • Check Wh ratings and airline rules before travel.
  • Recycle retired lithium packs separately.
