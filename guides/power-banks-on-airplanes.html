Power banks on airplanes: understand Wh and carry-on rules

Know the U.S. FAA baseline, then verify the actual airline and route.

ChargeFolio Editorial DeskReviewed 11 Nov 2026327 core words

Wh is the relevant size measure

Airline lithium-ion battery limits are expressed in watt-hours rather than headline mAh alone. For a product with a marked nominal voltage, Wh = V × (mAh ÷ 1,000). A 20,000 mAh pack at 3.7 V is approximately 74 Wh; one with a different nominal voltage may be in another category.

Use the manufacturer marking first. If the rating is ambiguous, check its specifications rather than inventing the voltage.

Carry-on versus checked luggage

The FAA requires spare lithium-ion batteries and power banks in carry-on baggage, protected from accidental damage and terminal short circuits. They are not permitted in checked baggage as spare batteries. If your carry-on is gate checked, remove power banks and spare batteries and keep them with you in the cabin.

This is a general U.S. framework, not a universal permission for every airline, route, country or device type.

Capacity categories and approval

Under the FAA passenger guidance, packs rated 0–100 Wh generally fit the ordinary spare-battery category. Packs 101–160 Wh require airline approval and are limited to a maximum of two larger spares per passenger. Larger lithium-ion spare batteries are not permitted as ordinary passenger baggage.

Airlines may impose tighter requirements and operational restrictions on use, number and storage. Check the carrier policy close to travel; do not rely on a calculator as an airline authorization.

Condition matters as much as capacity

Damaged, defective or recalled batteries can be prohibited regardless of capacity. Keep the pack protected from crush and shorts, carry it where it is accessible, and alert crew immediately if any battery overheats, smokes or swells in flight.

Our Wh calculator can help interpret a label. It does not verify airworthiness, airline approval, product authenticity or recall status.

Questions readers ask

Is a 100 Wh power bank automatically accepted on every flight?

No. The FAA provides general U.S. thresholds, but airlines and foreign authorities may apply stricter restrictions to number, location or use. The pack must also be in acceptable condition and properly protected. Check the specific airline and itinerary shortly before departure.

How do I handle a carry-on bag that is gate checked?

Remove power banks and other spare lithium-ion batteries before handing over the bag, and keep them in the cabin as required. Protect exposed terminals from short circuits and make sure the items remain accessible. Ask airline staff if unsure of the current procedure.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A 27,000 mAh pack at a stated 3.7 V is about 99.9 Wh and near a common U.S. threshold; rounding, labeling and airline-specific policies still need independent verification.

Practical checklist

  • Calculate Wh from an actual rated voltage.
  • Keep spare packs in carry-on only.
  • Ask your airline about 101–160 Wh packs and other limits.
  • Never fly with a damaged or recalled pack.
