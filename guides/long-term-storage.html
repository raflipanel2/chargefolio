Long-term storage: what to do before putting a device away

Storing a tablet or spare laptop for months is different from charging it every evening.

ChargeFolio Editorial DeskReviewed 11 Nov 2026319 core words

Begin with the actual device

A tablet, cordless drill pack, rechargeable camera battery and portable power station may have different chemistry, protection circuitry and storage recommendations. Read the manufacturer instructions first. Do not apply phone-specific percentages to every rechargeable product.

For supported Apple devices, the company recommends around a half charge, power off, and a cool dry place for long-term storage. It also recommends checking and bringing long-stored devices to approximately 50% periodically, with additional detail for storage beyond six months.

Choose the location carefully

Look for a location away from direct sun, heat sources, moisture and high traffic where terminals could contact metal. Loose removable batteries should be protected from accidental short circuits according to the manufacturer. Do not leave devices charging underneath stored fabrics.

For e-bike batteries, large tool packs or battery stations, use specific manufacturer instructions and local fire guidance. Those products can have additional hazards and handling requirements.

Plan for a return to use

Label the stored item with a check date and the product name. When retrieving it, inspect the casing, contacts and charger for damage before attempting normal use. Do not charge a swollen, leaking, smoking or clearly damaged unit.

A battery that has deeply discharged may need the vendor’s service process. Avoid improvised “jump-start” wiring, direct cell charging or repeated unsafe attempts to wake it.

Keep a simple record

Your record needs a model, storage date, approximate last charge and next check date, not private device serial numbers. If the item is stored for an emergency, periodically verify readiness using the approved test method in the manual.

If a battery becomes unusable, use an authorized recycling route rather than normal household trash or curbside recycling for lithium-ion products.

Questions readers ask

Should stored devices be kept at 100%?

Many lithium-powered portable devices are better stored at a partial charge, but exact instructions vary by manufacturer and chemistry. For supported Apple devices, the published guidance suggests about half charge for long-term storage. Always use the actual product manual rather than applying one figure universally.

What if the stored device will not turn on?

First inspect for damage and review the vendor’s instructions for long-term discharge. Some systems have protective shutdown behavior or need approved service. Do not open the battery, bypass protection electronics or try improvised jump-start wiring. For any swelling or excessive heat, stop and seek help.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A rarely used tablet can be turned off and placed in a cool indoor cabinet with a calendar reminder; a large e-bike battery should follow its own manual instead.

Practical checklist

  • Find device-specific storage guidance.
  • Avoid heat, moisture and loose exposed terminals.
  • Set a dated check-in.
  • Never attempt to “revive” a visibly damaged lithium battery.
