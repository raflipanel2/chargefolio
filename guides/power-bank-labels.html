How to read a power-bank label before relying on it

Capacity, output power and travel classification are different measurements.

ChargeFolio Editorial DeskReviewed 11 Nov 2026366 core words

Locate the three specifications

Find nominal cell capacity in mAh, energy capacity in Wh, and output ratings such as 5 V at 3 A or a USB-C watt limit. The first two describe stored energy; the output ratings describe how the pack can deliver it. A product with high mAh is not automatically able to charge a power-hungry laptop.

Packaging may print several capacities for different voltage points. Prefer official manufacturer specifications and identify which is the internal cell rating and which is an output rating after conversion.

Estimate usable energy

To compare packs at different nominal voltages, convert to Wh using the labeled mAh and V. Then apply an assumed conversion efficiency separately if estimating energy delivered to a device. Efficiency varies with load and operating conditions and should not be presented as a guaranteed percentage.

Our Power Bank Planner lets you enter a device’s approximate Wh requirement and try multiple efficiency assumptions. A zero-to-full number is theoretical; charging while using the device changes the outcome.

Air travel is a separate question

Under FAA guidance for ordinary passenger travel, spare lithium-ion batteries and power banks must be carried in cabin baggage and protected from damage and short circuit. Common 0–100 Wh packs generally fit the listed size category; larger 101–160 Wh packs require carrier approval and face quantity limits. Airlines or countries may be stricter.

The printed Wh label helps airline staff verify the rating. Check the airline and route before travel, especially when connecting internationally.

Good questions before purchase

Can the pack deliver the power your laptop expects? Does it have a clear Wh rating? Is its claimed capacity expressed at a documented voltage? Can you locate a manufacturer manual and warranty contact? These questions matter more than a headline number alone.

For safety, avoid damaged or swollen packs and keep charging away from heat-trapping soft surfaces. Dispose of end-of-life lithium packs through an appropriate battery collection program.

Questions readers ask

Which specification matters for charging a laptop?

Two independent specifications matter: stored energy (Wh) affects how long the bank may help, while supported output wattage and protocols affect whether it can power or charge the laptop at all. A high-capacity bank with a low-power output may be unsuitable for a computer.

Why is the power-bank mAh number larger than phone mAh but fewer charges happen?

The pack’s rating often refers to its internal cells, while output conversion uses energy and the phone itself consumes power while charging. Multiply nominal mAh by nominal voltage to get Wh, apply realistic losses, then compare with the phone’s battery energy rather than mAh alone.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A labeled 20,000 mAh at 3.7 V is around 74 Wh nominal. It is not a 20,000 mAh supply at every USB output voltage, and it does not promise four full charges of any particular phone.

Practical checklist

  • Read mAh, nominal V, Wh and output W separately.
  • Confirm device-specific charging compatibility.
  • Check airline restrictions independently before a flight.
  • Avoid damaged packs and use proper recycling.
