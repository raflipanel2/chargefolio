Heat and battery aging: practical temperature habits

Heat matters, but the useful response is often a small change in where and how you charge.

ChargeFolio Editorial DeskReviewed 11 Nov 2026390 core words

Why heat deserves attention

Lithium-ion batteries age faster when exposed to prolonged high temperatures. Apple identifies ambient temperatures above 35°C as conditions that can permanently damage battery capacity for its supported devices. The exact operating limits of another product may differ, so its manufacturer instructions should be your first reference.

A phone feeling slightly warm while charging is not automatically an emergency. By contrast, unusual or escalating heat, deformation, smoke, a sharp odor or hissing calls for stopping use and following the manufacturer or emergency safety guidance.

Find avoidable heat sources

Look at surfaces and placement rather than just charging speed. A phone charging on bedding, a laptop blocked by a cushion, or a device left in direct sunlight can trap or accumulate heat. Move normal charging to a firm, clear surface with airflow and away from combustible clutter.

If a thick case causes a particular device to become unusually warm during charging, consult its manufacturer instructions. Apple advises removing certain cases in that situation. Do not put electronics into a refrigerator or freezer to cool them.

Workload changes the picture

Gaming, video export, navigation and high-brightness screens can generate heat at the same time the battery is charging. It may be sensible to reduce a demanding task or move to a cooler room when temperatures are high. Measure the improvement by observing normal operation, not by repeatedly touching or manipulating battery parts.

Cooling accessories should not obstruct vents, introduce moisture or require opening the battery. Safe physical conditions matter more than chasing a particular battery percentage.

Storage is different from use

For long-term storage, Apple recommends switching off applicable devices and leaving them approximately half charged in a cool, moisture-free location. This is not a universal prescription for all battery chemistries or equipment. A power tool pack, an e-bike or backup battery may have its own storage schedule.

Write down the model and storage date and set a check-in reminder. If a pack develops damage or swelling, do not charge it to test whether it still works.

Questions readers ask

Is a warm phone during navigation always faulty?

No. Navigation uses radios, screen and processing while charging can add heat. The relevant questions are whether temperature is unusually high for the device, whether there is an official warning, and whether the situation improves when moved away from sun and heavy workload.

Can I put a hot device in a refrigerator?

Do not use a refrigerator or freezer as a battery-cooling method. Condensation and extreme temperatures introduce other risks. Stop demanding tasks, remove the device from avoidable heat, use a manufacturer-approved resting place and follow specific alerts or safety instructions for abnormal overheating.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A laptop used on a hard desk beside an open vent is a safer starting point than the same laptop on a blanket in direct afternoon sun. Check that the laptop maker permits the setup.

Practical checklist

  • Keep chargers and devices away from direct sun and soft bedding.
  • Respect the device maker’s ambient-temperature limits.
  • Reduce heavy workloads if heat is excessive.
  • Treat abnormal swelling, smoke or hissing as a safety issue.
