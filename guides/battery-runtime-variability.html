Why battery runtime varies between estimates and real use

Capacity ratings are useful, but devices rarely draw a fixed number of watts.

ChargeFolio Editorial DeskReviewed 11 Nov 2026298 core words

Energy and power are different

Battery energy in Wh divided by an assumed constant load in W gives an illustrative runtime in hours. This simple arithmetic is useful for a flashlight or controlled load, but many devices vary their draw significantly with screen brightness, radio strength and application activity.

The rating on a battery is measured under specified conditions. It does not guarantee identical usable energy at all loads, temperatures or ages.

Losses and cutoffs matter

Power conversion, cabling and protective shutdown thresholds reduce energy actually delivered to the load. A pack may stop output while energy remains nominally stored, because protection circuits must keep the battery within safe operating limits.

Our Watt-hour Lab separates nominal energy from a selectable efficiency assumption. It does not diagnose the condition of a particular battery.

Create your own comparison

For ordinary, non-critical devices, choose one controlled scenario and measure the approximate duration under similar conditions across multiple runs. Document whether the screen, wireless connection, temperature and external attachments were the same.

If you are planning power for safety-critical, medical or emergency systems, seek manufacturer-approved or professional calculations with appropriate redundancy rather than relying on a consumer estimation webpage.

Interpret disagreement usefully

If a lamp powered by a USB pack runs for four hours rather than six, investigate assumed device watts and conversion losses before declaring the battery fraudulent. If the pack becomes hot, visibly changes shape, or behaves abnormally, stop the experiment and seek service.

Use a range of efficiency and load values to understand uncertainty rather than presenting one decimal-heavy result as an exact promise.

Questions readers ask

Why is real runtime lower than the calculator says?

A load often varies over time; conversion circuits draw energy and protection electronics may stop output before every nominal Wh is available. Temperature, battery age and screen or radio use add differences. Try a plausible range of load and efficiency values rather than assuming the estimate is exact.

Can I size an emergency medical power supply with this calculator?

Do not rely on a simple consumer estimator for medical or life-safety equipment. Device makers and qualified professionals can establish supported runtime, failover behavior, reserve margins and required testing. The tools here are for educational comparison of ordinary, non-critical loads.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A nominal 50 Wh battery with 85% usable-energy assumption and 10 W average load gives 4.25 hours as a model—not necessarily the observed runtime.

Practical checklist

  • Use Wh for energy and W for load.
  • Choose a realistic load range.
  • Keep efficiency an explicit assumption.
  • Never use a simple web estimate for critical equipment.
